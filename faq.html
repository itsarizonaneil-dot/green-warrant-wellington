Self-Containment Certification FAQ

Straight answers about green warrants, cost, the fixed toilet rule, renewal, and how booking works.

Blue warrants were issued under the previous self-containment scheme. From 7 June 2026, blue warrants are no longer valid nationwide — every vehicle used for freedom camping needs a current green warrant issued under the Self-Contained Vehicles Regulations 2023.

Freedom camping without valid self-containment certification can result in a $400 fine.

A green warrant is valid for 4 years from the date of issue, after which the vehicle needs a revalidation inspection. See our renewal page for details.

No. A permanently fixed toilet is now required — portable toilets no longer qualify a vehicle as self-contained. If your vehicle still has one, see our retrofit & plumbing page.

Typical certification cost is NZ$183–280, including the NZ$120 PGDB levy, before any retrofit work your vehicle might need.

No. Green Warrant Wellington connects you with a licensed PGDB-authorised Certification Authority who carries out the inspection and issues certification. We don't perform inspections or issue certificates ourselves.

Make sure your vehicle has a permanently fixed toilet and adequate fresh, grey and black water storage, then book an inspection with a PGDB-authorised Certification Authority. If your vehicle needs retrofit work first, that should be arranged before booking the inspection.

Inspectors check the fixed toilet, fresh water tank capacity and fittings, and grey/black water storage and outlets, against the standard in the Self-Contained Vehicles Regulations 2023.
