Wellington City

As the North Island terminus for the Interislander ferry to Picton, Wellington sees two kinds of demand: resident campervan and motorhome owners needing first-time certification or renewal, and South-Island-bound travellers who need to be certified before they cross. If you're booking a ferry crossing, get your inspection sorted a few days ahead rather than the morning of — Certification Authority slots around the ferry terminal fill up in peak season.

Lower Hutt & Wainuiomata

Lower Hutt and Wainuiomata owners are close enough to central Wellington that inspection slots serving the wider Wellington region are a short drive rather than a special trip. This is also renewal territory — a lot of the self-contained vehicles that were first certified in the Hutt Valley in the early years of the scheme are now approaching their 4-year revalidation date, so if it's been a while since your last inspection, it's worth checking your certificate date.

Porirua

Porirua sits on the route north out of Wellington, so it's a natural stop for owners heading up the Kāpiti Coast or further afield who want certification sorted before setting off rather than partway through a trip. It's also home to a growing number of resident motorhome owners who freedom camp regionally on weekends — the $400 fine for freedom camping without valid certification applies here the same as anywhere else in New Zealand.

Kāpiti Coast

The Kāpiti Coast is one of the more popular stretches for holidaying motorhome owners in the wider region, with a steady stream of vehicles passing through on their way further north. That popularity also means it's a common spot for owners to discover their certification has lapsed or their portable-toilet setup no longer qualifies under the current fixed-toilet requirement — worth checking before you set off rather than finding out at a freedom camping site.

Upper Hutt

Upper Hutt has a higher concentration of campgrounds and holiday parks nearby than most of the region, which means a lot of local owners split their time between powered sites and genuine freedom camping — and only the second kind needs a valid green warrant. If you mostly use powered campgrounds but occasionally freedom camp, it's still worth getting certified so you're not caught out on a last-minute trip.
